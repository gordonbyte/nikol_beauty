## Coming-soon build process written down, and the icon toolkit moved into the repo

- Randell wants to run **five Claude Code sessions in parallel** to get through the remaining
  coming-soon pages, so the process we have been working out page by page needed to exist
  somewhere a fresh session can read it.
- **`docs/coming-soon-playbook.md`** — the whole process: the hard rules, what a `coming_soon_page`
  metaobject is made of, what to read before making anything, the per-section sizing rules that
  actually bite (skin-safe slot trims 4.9% each side; alternating rows are aspect-free so every row
  needs the same ratio; the banner stretches a square file to ~580x376), the photo-editing methods
  (derive geometry from pixel scans, inpainting rules, background lifting, pan repainting), icons,
  the staging route into Shopify Files, storefront verification, and how to finish.
- **`docs/coming-soon-prompt.md`** — the paste-able prompt with four fields to fill, plus the
  parallel-run notes and a suggested first batch of five.
- **`tools/icon-badges/`** — `proposed.cjs` (43 icons) and `render-proposed.cjs` copied out of the
  session scratchpad, which is where they had been living. Without this a fresh session could not
  reproduce the badge family at all and would re-derive the geometry badly. Verified it runs from
  its new home: renders all 42, including the two built this week (`sensitive-eye-safe-shield`,
  `vivid-long-wearing-color-clock`). Generated output goes to `proposed/` and is not kept.
- **Parallel-safety rules are the substance of it**, not an afterthought: one product per session;
  never touch an image or metaobject shared with another product (the four eyeshadow step photos
  are one upload used by all seven palettes); unique, never-reused theme staging names because
  asset URLs are not versioned and a reused name serves cached bytes; a `mkdir` lock around
  `shopify theme push`; a changelog file per product rather than five sessions appending to one;
  KAN-211 comments prefixed with the product name; expect and back off from API throttling.
- No store or theme changes in this entry — documentation and tooling only.

## All eight lipsticks — 56 empty image slots filled, each shade repainted per product (DEV)

- Randell asked for every lipstick page to show its own shade, on Nikol and on the bullet, using
  Higgsfield to change the colour. Multi-lipstick frames were to show the current product as the
  primary one.
- **The folder had more in it than expected.** `Pictures/Coming soon/lips/lip-stick` holds ten
  COLORS graphics: a range chart, arm swatches, and — the find — **one real photograph per shade**
  showing the bullet laying colour on the lip and the finished lips beneath it, with a flat colour
  chip naming the shade. All eight. So "how it looks on lips" needed no invention at all.
- **Measured all eight shades three ways** off those graphics — the chip, the finished lower lip
  and the bullet — as per-channel medians, medians because the lip's specular highlights drag a
  mean upward. Beach House for instance: chip #af3c28, on lips #c15039, bullet #a84b30.
- **Ran Higgsfield as asked, and it failed the job.** gpt_image_2 at 2k/high, 6.5 credits, one
  edit of q2101 to Beach House against the same edit done in Lab. It produced a coral lip, but it
  came back **a different woman**: eyes, brows, nose and jawline redrawn, skin smoothed, lip shape
  changed. It also reframed the shot and redrew the product — the barrel's black and silver bands
  the wrong widths, a blunter bullet, a different ring. The root cause is not the prompt; any
  generative edit regenerates pixels, and these are photographs of the founder's face. Showed
  Randell both and he took the Lab route.
- **The Lab recolour is the eyeshadow pan method pointed at lips.** Shading kept, a and b shifted
  so the average hue and chroma land on the measured target. Lips are mostly highlight and texture,
  which a flat fill destroys and this preserves. Her face comes out pixel-identical.
- **The mask separates lip from skin on chroma, not lightness** — measured on q2101 the lips run
  0.67-0.78 saturation and 114-148 redness against skin at 0.29-0.42 and 57-74. The first attempt
  used 0.33/28, far too loose, and painted half her cheek. Thresholds came from a probe across the
  mouth, not a guess.
- **Regions were auto-located rather than eyeballed per photo**: the same chroma gate, connected
  components, largest first — the mouth is always one of the top two blobs and the bullet the
  other. Verified all five in one overlay. It also flagged what to avoid: her nails are red in two
  frames and would otherwise have been repainted.
- Built 56 images: five Nikol photographs recoloured across eight shades (40) plus two crops per
  shade out of that shade's own graphic (16). Steps are 1.796, rows square, banner 3:2 — both
  sections render width 100% / height auto, so a set with mixed ratios stops lining up.
- **Switched the output to JPEG.** As PNG the 56 files came to 230MB; at quality 0.92 they are
  28MB, for photographs Shopify re-encodes on serve anyway.
- **Two crop corrections after looking at the rendered set**: step 1 began 8px above her upper lip
  and clipped it, so the frame was widened to hold the mouth and the bullet at once.
